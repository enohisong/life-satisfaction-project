Project Overview

What this project set out to answer

Life satisfaction is one of psychology's most studied constructs — yet predicting it computationally, especially with a novel dataset, remains an open challenge. This thesis applied two contrasting machine learning architectures to the LISS longitudinal survey panel to find out which predictors matter most, and how those priorities shift across age groups.

Research Questions

RQ1 — To what extent can TabNet and Gradient Boosting predict life satisfaction from personality, well-being, and leisure features?

RQ2 — What is the relative influence of each feature category?

SubQ — How does that influence vary across age groups?

Dataset

The LISS Panel (Longitudinal Internet Studies for Social Sciences), run by CentERdata at Tilburg University. Three waves merged: background variables, Social Integration (Wave 13), and Personality (Wave 15).

Starting from 630 raw variables, preprocessing reduced to 411 features covering 4,058 Dutch individuals.

Model Comparison

Gradient Boosting vs TabNet

Both models underwent extensive hyperparameter tuning via Grid Search with 5-fold cross-validation. The Gradient Boosting Regressor outperformed TabNet on all metrics — likely due to better alignment with the structured tabular nature of the LISS dataset.

Key Findings

What the data revealed

🧠
Well-being dominates
Mood, happiness, optimism and trust account for 79% of predictive power — far outweighing personality or leisure. Interventions focused on mental well-being will have the greatest impact on life satisfaction.
📊
GBR beats deep learning
Despite extensive tuning, TabNet (R²=39.5%) fell short of Gradient Boosting (R²=49.9%). Ensemble tree methods remain superior on structured psychometric tabular data.
🔬
Neuroticism as key trait
Among personality traits, neuroticism shows the strongest correlation with life satisfaction — consistent with Prati (2022) but diverging from Shen et al. (2023) who found conscientiousness dominant.
✅
No heteroscedasticity
Breusch-Pagan test returned p=0.48, confirming constant variance in residuals. The GBR model satisfies key regression assumptions, adding confidence to its predictions.
📈
Happiness grows with age
Happiness importance peaks in the senior age group (56–100), aligning with Prati (2022). Seniors also show decreased neuroticism, which contributes to higher life satisfaction in later life.
🌐
Novel dataset application
First application of TabNet to the LISS dataset for life satisfaction prediction. Establishes a reproducible baseline for future researchers using this dataset in psychological prediction tasks.
Age Interaction Analysis

Life satisfaction priorities shift with age

By segmenting the dataset into three life stages, the GBR model reveals how the relative importance of predictors evolves — enabling age-targeted interventions.

Young adults
18 – 35
  • Online activities & digital engagement
  • Travel and places visited
  • Extraversion & openness to experience
  • Self-esteem as key well-being driver
  • Conscientiousness moderately influential
Middle-aged adults
36 – 55
  • Balanced leisure across all activities
  • Recreational organisations & travel
  • Agreeableness & conscientiousness rise
  • Self-esteem tied to career & social status
  • Increased financial stability enables diverse experiences
Seniors
56 – 100
  • Family time becomes paramount
  • Leisure time (retirement-driven)
  • Decreased neuroticism → higher satisfaction
  • Trust, mood & happiness dominate
  • Social relationships and support central
Average feature scores by age group — personality traits interaction
Extraversion: Young 0.28, Mid 0.18, Senior 0.05. Neuroticism: Young 0.22, Mid 0.15, Senior -0.05. Agreeableness: Young 0.20, Mid 0.22, Senior 0.10.
